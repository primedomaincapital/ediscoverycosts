Market data · eDiscovery market size, review costs, and document volumes

The second invoice in every lawsuit

Discovery is where litigation budgets go to be surprised. The evidence lives in inboxes, chat logs, and phones, and the bill for finding it can rival the cost of arguing the case. Public market studies disagree on the total size of the eDiscovery market by billions — and that disagreement itself is informative, because it turns on what counts as eDiscovery in the first place.

The arc from $18,000 per gigabyte in 2012 to single-digit or tens-of-dollars per gigabyte today is the real story: review technology compressed unit costs while data volumes grew far faster than budgets. ComplexDiscovery's reconciled view makes the tension explicit — data growth outrunning market growth by a wide margin through 2030, which means every dollar of review spend has to cover more documents each year. The firms that win that arithmetic are the ones treating collection discipline and early case assessment as cost controls, not administrative chores.

Methodology

Included: public market-size summaries from three research publishers, a reconciled task-spend analysis, the RAND 2012 per-gigabyte benchmark, and a 2025 industry pricing survey. Excluded: paywalled report tables, vendor rate cards, and any court-published per-case cost dataset — none was verified this session. Market totals differ because firms define the market differently (software only vs software plus services); the page shows the range rather than picking one.
